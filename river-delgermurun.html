The river

Water, land and people

The Delgermurun runs clear and fast out of the mountains of northern Khuvsgul, through larch forest and open valleys, before it cuts into a deep rock canyon. Together with the Ider it forms the Selenge, which flows north to Lake Baikal. The water is cold, clean and easy to read — riffles, long glides and boulder pools that hold taimen, lenok and grayling.

We float it the way we float every river: no shuttles. Each day you fish new water from a drift boat while the crew moves camp ahead, so the tents are up and dinner is on when you arrive. Summer light lasts late, so the evening rise is yours.

What makes it special

Landscape, wildlife, culture

01

Landscape

Rock canyon walls, larch forest and wide grassy valleys — the river changes character almost every day of the float.

02

Wildlife

You may see red and roe deer along the banks, eagles and black kites overhead, and cranes feeding in the open valleys.

03

Culture

Herding families spend the summer along these valleys with their gers and livestock. Further north lies reindeer-herding country.
